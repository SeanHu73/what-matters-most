Part One
The Tallest
Mountain

You "know" Mount Everest is the tallest mountain.
You are wrong. Or right. Depending on the ruler.

There are three contestants. None of this is standardized. We are vibing.

Everest
Contestant #1
Mount Everest
The People's Champion
  • 8,848.86 m above sea level
  • That number took Nepal and China until December 2020 to agree on
  • Wins on: "highest point above sea level"
  • That's it. That's the whole claim.
📷 Photo: Mount Everest
Mauna Kea
Contestant #2
Mauna Kea
The Underdog (Literally)
  • 4,205 m above sea level (embarrassing in this lineup)
  • ~10,210 m from its base on the Pacific Ocean floor to summit
  • Wins on: "base to peak." Beats Everest by 1.4 km.
  • 60% of it is underwater, sulking
  • Fun bonus: Mauna Kea's weight has pushed the ocean crust down 8 km. The mountain is sinking into the planet. Even Mauna Kea cannot handle Mauna Kea.
Chimborazo
Contestant #3
Mount Chimborazo
The Loophole Guy
  • Only 6,263 m above sea level
  • BUT: Earth bulges at the equator (it's an oblate spheroid)
  • Chimborazo sits 1° south of the equator, right on the bulge
  • From Earth's center, its summit is 2,072 m farther out than Everest's
  • Wins on: "closest point on Earth's surface to outer space"
  • The mountain equivalent of "well, technically…"
So Why Isn't This Standardized?
  • Sea level is the convention for aviation, weather, mapping → mountains inherited it
  • Awkward bit: sea level itself is fake
  • The ocean isn't level. The Atlantic is ~1 m lower on one side of the Gulf Stream
  • What we actually use: the geoid, a math model of what sea level would be without weather, tides, and currents
  • The geoid looks like a lumpy potato
  • We measure mountains against an imaginary potato
The Verdict
Everest= highest Mauna Kea= tallest Chimborazo= furthest

All correct. All wrong. None of this matters.

Also Olympus Mons on Mars is 21.9 km tall,
more than 2× any of these. Stay humble.

Part Three
Glyphosate

What even is it?

What Even Is It
  • Glyphosate = active ingredient in Roundup. Invented 1974.
  • Kills weeds by blocking the shikimate pathway
  • Humans and animals don't have this pathway
  • It's like trying to break a lock you don't own
Why It's Everywhere
  • Supports ~75% of US cropland
  • Most-used herbicide on Earth
  • Low mammalian toxicity (comparable to table salt; paraquat, which it replaced, kills you in a teaspoon)
  • Enabled no-till farming, which is the actual headline
The No-Till Receipts
  • US cropland erosion rates ↓ ~33% since 1982
  • Glyphosate-tolerant soybean adoption alone cut erosion by 27 million tons/year
  • Saves ~60 million gallons of diesel/year in the US
  • ~500,000 tons of CO₂ avoided per year
  • If you care about soil and carbon, glyphosate is doing the work
March 2015
IARC classifies glyphosate as
"probably carcinogenic"
(Group 2A)

Cue: panic, lawsuits, billions in Bayer settlements,
your aunt's WhatsApp forwards

The Most Important Distinction
Nobody Understands
Hazard

"Could this cause harm in some dose, somewhere?"

Risk

"Will the amount you actually encounter hurt you?"

The sun is a Group 1 carcinogen. The sun is still operational.

Glyphosate's Group 2A Friends

Group 2A — Probably Carcinogenic

  • Red meat
  • Working night shifts
  • Being a hairdresser
  • Drinking beverages above 65°C

Group 1 — Definitely Carcinogenic

  • Tobacco
  • Asbestos
  • Alcohol
  • Bacon
  • Outdoor air pollution

Bacon is in a higher cancer category than glyphosate. Bacon.

The IARC vs. EPA Beef
  • Almost every major regulator disagrees with IARC: EPA, EFSA, ECHA, Health Canada, Australian regulator, Japanese Food Safety Commission, WHO/FAO
  • IARC reviewed 8 animal studies; EPA reviewed 15
  • IARC uses only published studies (rigorous); regulators include industry data (more complete)
  • IARC asks "any cancer link, anywhere?"; regulators ask "at actual exposure levels?"
  • Both defensible. Real scientific disagreement, not a conspiracy.
Where There IS A Real Concern
  • Decades of unprotected occupational spraying: modest increase in non-Hodgkin lymphoma risk
  • Wear gloves. Read the label.
  • Consumer exposure from food? Orders of magnitude below anything that's shown an effect.
  • You'd have to eat absolutely deranged quantities of bread.
What Happens If We Ban It
  • Farmers go back to tilling → more erosion, more carbon released
  • Or switch to older, more toxic herbicides we deliberately moved away from
  • Food prices ↑ (a 10% tax alone = $98M/year in losses for US corn)
  • Imports from countries with weaker regulation → you eat it anyway
The Boring Correct Take
Glyphosate is in the same cancer category as your barber.

It enabled the soil conservation movement.
We're regulating it out of existence based on vibes.

"Use it carefully, watch the data."

Which is unfortunately the take nobody will retweet.
